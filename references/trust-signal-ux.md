# Trust Signal UX

Why a green status badge converts customers.

## The conversion math

Stripe published in 2022 that adding a "secure checkout" badge bumped conversion 5-12% on average. A live "47/47 systems passing" badge — verifiable, timestamped, real — outperforms any static badge.

Customers don't trust copy. They trust evidence.

## Three placements that move the needle

### 1. Pricing page footer

Right above the CTA. Format:

```
[ green dot ] All systems operational · last checked 2 min ago
```

Effect: removes objection ("what if it breaks?") at the exact moment they're deciding to buy.

### 2. Dashboard widget (logged-in users)

Inside the admin/account dashboard. Format: full Trust Grid widget.

Effect: signals "we know what we're doing" — reduces support tickets like "is this thing broken?"

### 3. Public /status page

Linked from footer. Same widget, full-screen.

Effect: when something does break, you give customers a place to look BEFORE they tweet.

## Copy that works

| Bad | Good |
|---|---|
| "Trust us, it works" | "47/47 systems passing · last checked 2 min ago" |
| "100% uptime" | "0 incidents in last 30 days" |
| "Secure" | "All payment flows tested every 6 hours" |

## Color rules

- **Green** = all passing. Use sparingly so it means something.
- **Yellow** = 1-2 failures or stale data (> 6 hours). Honest, not alarming.
- **Red** = active outage. Tell customers what's broken and ETA.

Never green-wash. If something is failing, show it as failing. The credibility you earn from honesty is worth more than the temporary loss from showing red.

## The Italo principle

> "If I can't trust the UI, why would my customers trust anything else?"

Customers buy from companies they trust. A live green badge says "we test our own product." A red badge says "we know what's broken and we're fixing it." Both build trust. Hiding the truth destroys it.
